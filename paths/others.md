# 🧭 Other Paths in Computer Science

Computer Science is not limited to writing software or becoming a traditional software engineer.

There are several paths where technical knowledge combines with areas such as product development, design, communication, research, documentation, and community building.

This section covers:

- Product Management
- UX/UI Design
- Technical Writing
- Developer Relations
- Computer Science Research

These paths can also overlap with software engineering, AI/ML, data, cloud, cybersecurity, and systems.

# 📦 Product Management

Product Management focuses on understanding problems, users, business requirements, and technology to help build useful products.

A Product Manager does not simply "manage developers."

The role involves understanding:

- What problem should be solved?
- Who has the problem?
- Why does it matter?
- What should be built?
- What should be built first?
- How will success be measured?

---

## 🧭 What Does a Product Manager Do?

Typical activities include:

- Understanding user problems
- Conducting user research
- Defining product requirements
- Creating product roadmaps
- Prioritizing features
- Writing product requirements
- Working with engineers
- Working with designers
- Analyzing product data
- Measuring product outcomes
- Communicating product decisions

---

## 🧱 Useful Foundations

Learn:

- Problem solving
- Communication
- Basic software development
- Databases
- APIs
- Basic system design
- Data analysis
- User research
- Product metrics
- Business fundamentals

You don't need to become an expert programmer, but understanding technology makes communication with engineering teams much easier.

# 🔬 Computer Science Research

Research focuses on creating or discovering new knowledge.

Research can involve:

- Algorithms
- Artificial Intelligence
- Machine Learning
- Computer Vision
- Natural Language Processing
- Systems
- Networks
- Security
- Databases
- Human-Computer Interaction
- Robotics
- Theoretical Computer Science

---

## 🧭 What Does a Researcher Do?

A research workflow often looks like:

```text
Identify a Problem
       ↓
Study Existing Work
       ↓
Find a Research Gap
       ↓
Form a Hypothesis
       ↓
Design Method
       ↓
Implement / Experiment
       ↓
Collect Results
       ↓
Analyze Results
       ↓
Compare With Existing Work
       ↓
Communicate Findings
```

Research is not simply reading papers.

You often need to question existing approaches and produce evidence.

---

## 🧱 Foundations

Depending on the research area, useful foundations include:

- Mathematics
- Statistics
- Algorithms
- Data Structures
- Programming
- Experimental methodology
- Scientific writing
- Data analysis

The required depth depends heavily on the research field.

```text

  ↓

  ↓
Analysis
  ↓
```

```text
                    Computer Science
                           │
          ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
          ↓                ↓                ↓
       Product           Design          Technology
          │                │                │
          ↓                ↓                ↓

                           ↓
                    Developer Relations
                           │

                      Community
                           │
```

These are not isolated careers.

For example:

- A Product Manager works with engineers and designers.
- A UX Designer works with product and engineering teams.
- A Technical Writer works with developers and users.
- A DevRel professional works with developers and technology teams.
- A Researcher may collaborate with engineers to implement experiments.

# 🧭 Choosing an Exploration Direction

Instead of asking:

> "Which career is the best?"

Ask:

> "Which type of problems do I enjoy solving?"

### If you enjoy...

**Understanding users and problems**

→ Product

**Designing experiences**

→ UX/UI

**Explaining technical concepts**

→ Technical Writing

**Teaching, communities, and technology**

→ Developer Relations

**Asking unanswered questions**

→ Research

**Building software**

→ Software Engineering

**Working with data**

→ Data

**Building intelligent systems**

→ AI & ML

**Infrastructure and large-scale systems**

→ Cloud & Infrastructure

**Understanding systems and how computers work**

→ Systems & Core Computing

**Protecting systems**

→ Cybersecurity

These interests can overlap.
